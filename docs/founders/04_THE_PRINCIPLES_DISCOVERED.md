# The Principles Discovered

Throughout the journey of building Impande, we did not just write code; we uncovered fundamental philosophies about how memory and truth should be handled. These are the core principles that were discovered along the way. They should continue to evolve as experience grows, but their essence must remain intact.

### 1. Truth before convenience.
A system built for speed will sacrifice accuracy. A system built for comfort will hide difficult facts. Impande is built for truth. It is deliberately strict, requiring proper authorization and immutable logging, because convenience is the enemy of preservation.

### 2. Preserve uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.
If a piece of evidence is incomplete, or if a date is unknown, the system must record that uncertainty. It is far better to preserve an honest gap in history than to fill it with a confident lie.

### 3. Every person deserves dignity.
The platform is not reserved for the famous, the wealthy, or the powerful. The quiet sacrifices of mothers, taxi drivers, vendors, and teachers are the foundation of civilization. Impande exists to ensure their dignity is visible and recorded.

### 4. Service deserves remembrance.
Those who build, care, provide, and sacrifice without applause are the true architects of continuity. The system must honor those who serve.

### 5. Technology should serve people.
Technology is a tool, not a master. It must never reduce a human life to a metric, a trend, or a statistical anomaly. The algorithms must remain subordinate to human meaning.

### 6. Heritage belongs to future generations as well as the present.
We do not own this history; we are merely stewarding it for the children who are not yet born. Every decision made on this platform must be evaluated by asking: "Will this serve the generation that opens this archive fifty years from now?"
