## Purpose

This chapter preserves the known circumstances surrounding the birth of Gift
Nkosinathi Chisali and the first years of his life.

Its purpose is to document the convergence of the Chisali and Nkambule family
streams and preserve the earliest continuity records available through family
testimony and memory.

The chapter does not yet cover school, later hardship, or the Carolina years.

## Convergence of the Family Streams

During her youth, Ntombifuthi Florance Nkambule frequently visited her siblings
and relatives in Mooiplaas, Nhlazatje, Mpumalanga.

During this period she met Owen Sipho Chisali.

A relationship developed between them and eventually resulted in a pregnancy.

According to family testimony, Ntombifuthi was approximately eighteen years old
and progressing through secondary school when the pregnancy became known to her
family.

The news was not well received.

Family testimony states that Samuel Mehlwana Nkambule became angry and that
significant family conflict followed.

## Early Caregiving Period

Following the birth, Ntombifuthi sought to complete her schooling.

Family testimony indicates that Gift spent part of his earliest years under the
care of his paternal grandmother while his mother completed Grade 12.

After completion of her studies, Gift and his mother returned to the maternal
family environment.

This period may be understood as the first continuity bridge between the
paternal and maternal households. This bridge language is interpretive.

## Return to the Maternal Household

Gift's earliest remembered family environment included:

- Samuel Mehlwana Nkambule;
- three maternal uncles;
- two maternal aunts;
- his mother.

At this stage, most male family members were frequently away for work
opportunities.

The household therefore depended heavily upon family cooperation and mutual
support.

One of the maternal aunts, Rose Miriam Nkambule, worked as a nurse and would
later play a significant role in Gift's educational journey.

## Why This Chapter Matters

For a future reader, this chapter establishes that Gift's arrival did not occur
inside one isolated household. His earliest continuity depended on movement
between paternal and maternal family care while his mother continued her
education.

The testimony therefore preserves more than a birth event. It preserves an
early example of opportunity being carried through family cooperation,
caregiving, sacrifice, and return.

This meaning is a composite interpretation derived from the chapter's family
testimony. It does not claim that every person involved understood the period
in these terms at the time.
