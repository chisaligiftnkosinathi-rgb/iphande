## Part I: Observation from Significant Figures
*How a mathematical idea inspired thinking about historical continuity.*

In mathematics and science, significant figures are the digits that matter—the ones that carry meaning, precision, and truth. When we first asked about significant figures, it looked like a mathematics question. It wasn't. It was really another way of asking: **"Who gets counted?"** That question has appeared throughout every season of life—from a child, to a scientist, to a software engineer, to a steward. The mathematics became a doorway into a much deeper philosophical question.

## Part II: The Continuity Hypothesis
*The symbolic hypothesis regarding continuity and human history.*

**Hypothesis:** Black people stand as continuity’s significant figures—digits of memory, covenant, and resilience. 

**Note:** *This hypothesis is intended as a metaphorical framework and should be interpreted with care, especially when making broad claims about groups of people. It frames how a civilization's truth is anchored by the communities whose presence ensures accuracy in the story of humanity.*

- **Presence matters:** They are essential digits in humanity’s equation—their presence carries meaning.
- **Continuity is identity:** Culture, language, and resilience are preserved across generations.
- **No calculation without us:** Leaving out significant figures distorts a number; leaving out these voices distorts civilization’s truth.
- **Precision in heritage:** Every contribution adds exactness to humanity’s record.
- **Resilience as accuracy:** Survival through disruption proves the accuracy of their role in history.

## Part III: The Continuity Constant

```text
C = M + L + R + I + S
```

Where:
- **M (Memory):** Memory carried forward faithfully.
- **L (Lineage):** Lineage and succession handed to the next generation.
- **R (Resilience):** Resilience through disruption and oppression.
- **I (Integration):** Integration of ancient truths with modern realities.
- **S (Stewardship):** Stewardship covenant—treating inheritance as sacred, not just possession.
