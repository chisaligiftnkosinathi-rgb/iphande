# Mission 000: CIPC Foundation
**Archive class:** `PROTECTED` / `BUSINESS-KNOWLEDGE`

## Objective
Understand the life of a South African company from the moment it is born, establishing the universal baseline for the Phanda business domain.

---

## 1. What is CIPC?
*The Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) is the environment that gives companies legal existence in South Africa.*

- **Why was CIPC created?**  
  CIPC was established by the Companies Act, 2008 (Act No. 71 of 2008) as a juristic person to function as an organ of state. It was created by merging the Companies and Intellectual Property Registration Office (CIPRO) and the Office of Companies and Intellectual Property Enforcement (OCIPE).
- **What problem was it solving?**  
  It solved the problem of fragmented and outdated corporate registration (previously governed by the 1973 Act). It was designed to simplify the registration of businesses, make it easier for small businesses to enter the formal economy, and centralize the enforcement of company law to protect stakeholders and investors.
- **What does it regulate?**  
  It regulates the registration of Companies, Co-operatives, and Intellectual Property Rights (trademarks, patents, designs, and copyright).
- **What services does it provide?**  
  Maintenance of accurate, up-to-date information on registered entities, education on compliance, and enforcement of the Companies Act.

## 2. The Legal Life Cycle of a Company
*From birth to closure.*

1. **Reservation:** Reserving a unique identity (Name Reservation).
2. **Birth (Inception):** Registration of the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) and issuing of the Registration Certificate (COR14.3).
3. **Active Compliance:** Maintenance of legal standing via Annual Returns (submitted yearly on the anniversary of inception).
4. **Life Events:** Updating the state when reality changes (New directors, address shifts).
5. **Dormancy/Closure:** Deregistration (due to non-compliance) or voluntary Liquidation.
6. **Resurrection:** Reinstatement of a deregistered company.

---

## 3. Which Events Does CIPC Record?
*CIPC is the ledger of corporate truth. It records:*
- Inception (Registration)
- Director appointments and resignations (COR39)
- Registered address changes (COR21.1)
- Name changes (COR9.4 / COR14.3)
- Annual Returns (Financial Accountability Supplements or Audited Financial Statements)
- Auditor or Company Secretary changes (COR44)
- Business Rescue proceedings
- Deregistration and Reinstatements

## 4. Which Events Does CIPC NOT Record?
*CIPC does not track operational or financial health.*
- Tax payments, VAT registration, or PAYE (This is SARS).
- Hiring or firing of employees (This is Labour/UIF).
- Opening of business bank accounts (This is the Banking sector).
- Daily operational contracts and invoices.
- Procurement and tender awards (This is Treasury/CSD).

---

## 5. Which Events Begin at CIPC but Continue Elsewhere?
*CIPC is the trigger for the rest of the ecosystem.*

- **Company Registration:** Completing registration at CIPC automatically triggers SARS to issue an Income Tax number.
- **Annual Returns:** Failing to file Annual Returns at CIPC leads to deregistration, which freezes bank accounts and invalidates tender compliance on the Central Supplier Database (CSD).
- **Name/Director Changes:** A change recorded at CIPC must subsequently be updated at the Bank (FICA requirements) and SARS (Representative updates).

---

## 6. Synthesis: The CIPC Life Event
When a steward says, *"I want to start a business,"* CIPC is simply the first room they must walk through. Phanda's role is not just to file the CIPC paperwork, but to know that the moment CIPC says "Yes", SARS, the Bank, and the Department of Labour will immediately begin asking questions.
